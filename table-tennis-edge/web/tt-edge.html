Mispriced lines

Prices that beat fair value. Fair value comes from the other books' margin-free prices, the point model and, once players have enough history, the ratings. Edge is expected profit per unit staked. Stake is quarter Kelly, capped, as a share of bankroll. Select a row to see the whole match board.

Cross-book arbitrage

Best prices across books whose implied chances add up to under 100%. Splitting the stake as shown returns the same amount whoever wins. Soft-book arbs are often stale lines that get voided or cut, so check limits and retirement rules first.

Matches on the board

Fair match price from the blended point-win probability. Select a match to compare every book's price with fair value.

Match pricer

Enter a match-winner price pair (decimal, American like +150, or fractional like 5/2). The margin comes off, the fair price becomes a point-win probability, and every other market is priced from it. You can also enter the point probability directly.

Player ratings

Learned from every point in the results. P(point) v avg is the chance of winning a point against an average player. Small gaps matter: 52% per point is about 64% per best-of-5 match.

Walk-forward backtest

Replays past matches in time order. Ratings only know earlier results, bets take the opening prices, and closing prices measure closing-line value (CLV). CLV tells you sooner and more reliably than profit whether the method finds real mispricing.

Run the backtest to replay the history (about ten seconds for the demo).
